\section{Motivation and native learning graph}
The aim is to allocate a sequential edit across all eligible layers while
retaining native subject-position intervention. Every eligible layer is
present from initialization; concentration in one layer is allowed.
The design addresses two distinct problems: learning a subject-mediated
edit that generalizes beyond its training sentence, and realizing that
edit through a shared weight writer without excessive interference.
This is a proposed method, not a claim of measured improvement.

At batch entry, freeze the model $W_t$ and let $B$ denote the actual
logical batch size. For every request $r$ and eligible layer $l\in\mathcal L$,
initialize an absolute-coordinate trainable vector $\delta_{lr}=0$.
Write $D_l=[\delta_{l1},\ldots,\delta_{lB}]$. During the native joint
forward, inject at the same subject lookup and block-output site as the
native implementation:
\[
 h_{lrc}[s_{rc}]\leftarrow h_{lrc}[s_{rc}]+\delta_{lr}.
\]
The same request vector is used in that request's native rewrite contexts
and native KL input. All layers intervene in one forward graph; gradients
include propagation from lower to higher layers. Subject positions,
context construction, target tokens, attention masks, readouts, and
reductions remain those of the corresponding native model/benchmark
profile. Official paraphrases and neighborhood examples are never training
inputs. There is no additional reference corpus.

Let $a_{lr}=\norm{h^0_{lr}}>0$ be the frozen native anchor norm, using the
profile's canonical subject state at batch entry. With exact native
rewrite NLL $L^{\rm virt}_{N,r}$ and full-vocabulary native KL, define
\begin{align}
 L^{\rm virt}_{K,r}
 &=\KL\bigl(p^{\rm virt}_{K,r}(D)\,\Vert\,p^0_{K,r}\bigr),\\
 J_{\rm native}(D)
 &=\frac1B\sum_{r=1}^{B}\left[
 L^{\rm virt}_{N,r}+\lambda_K L^{\rm virt}_{K,r}
 +\lambda_n\sum_{l\in\mathcal L}\frac{\norm{\delta_{lr}}}{a_{lr}^{2}}
 \right]. \label{eq:native}
\end{align}
Native NLL and KL can use different readout layers; neither is silently
replaced by the final head. After each Adam update, project each group
onto $\norm{\delta_{lr}}\leq c_{\rm native}a_{lr}$. Native $\lambda_K$,
$\lambda_n$, $c_{\rm native}$, and the learning-rate profile are recorded
per model. The nonsquared norm is in the loss, not an AdamW decay.

The next pages define writer-aware allocation costs and occasional
physical feedback. Neither substitutes an all-token weight-learning path
for the primary subject-injected native loss.

\section{Fixed entry writer and cheap interference geometry}
Let $K_l=[k^0_{lrc}]\in\RR^{d_{{\rm in},l}\times n}$ contain the full
set of entry subject keys, with one column per native rewrite context.
Let $\Omega=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_{rc})$, where
$\sum_c\alpha_{rc}=1$ for each request. Let
$Z\in\{0,1\}^{B\times n}$ indicate context ownership, so that
$Z\Omega Z^\top=I_B$. The initial profile gives the canonical context
weight $1/2$ and shares the remaining $1/2$ across the noncanonical
native contexts; any profile-specific alternative must be explicit.
Define
\begin{align}
 A_l&=\lambda_C C_{0l}+H_{tl}, &
 \bar K_l&=K_l\Omega Z^\top,\\
 P_l&=(A_l+K_l\Omega K_l^\top)^{-1}\bar K_l,
 & U_l(D)&=D_lP_l^\top. \label{eq:writer}
\end{align}
All inverses denote numerically checked solves. $C_0$ is the fixed
background key statistic and $H_t$ is sequential committed history.
Both are geometry statistics, not extra language-model training inputs.
The basis $P_l$ is built once at batch entry and frozen throughout the
batch. This is the exact minimizer, for given $D_l$, of
\[
 \frac12\norm{(UK_l-D_lZ)\Omega^{1/2}}_F^2
 +\frac12\tr(UA_lU^\top).
\]
Context second moments and cross-request off-diagonal terms are retained.
Solving only with averaged-key outer products is a different writer.

Two small $B\times B$ positive-semidefinite matrices permit inexpensive
costs throughout native optimization:
\begin{align}
 G_l&=P_l^\top A_lP_l,\\
 E_l&=(P_l^\top K_l-Z)\Omega(P_l^\top K_l-Z)^\top,\\
 G_l+E_l&=I_B-\bar K_l^\top P_l. \label{eq:identity}
\end{align}
Their meanings are exact for the frozen entry geometry:
\begin{align}
 \tr(D_lG_lD_l^\top)&=\tr(U_lA_lU_l^\top),\\
 \tr(D_lE_lD_l^\top)&=
 \norm{(U_lK_l-D_lZ)\Omega^{1/2}}_F^2.
\end{align}
Thus $G_l$ measures weighted write action on background/history keys,
and $E_l$ measures entry-key realization error. Neither alone proves
functional locality preservation. The identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:identity}
is a validation condition; cancellation-sensitive residual energies
should be checked using the explicit factors.

For fixed $\sigma_l=(B^{-1}\sum_r a_{lr}^2)^{1/2}$, define
\[
 g_l=\frac{\sqrt{\tr(D_lG_lD_l^\top)}}{\sqrt B\,\sigma_l},
 \qquad
 e_l=\frac{\sqrt{\tr(D_lE_lD_l^\top)}}{\sqrt B\,\sigma_l}.
\]
These dimensionless, batch-normalized costs expose cross-request coupling
through the full Gram matrices. They do not capture changing nonlinear
keys after lower-layer edits; the physical pulses address that gap.

\section{Two arms: freedom to concentrate versus a spread preference}
The arms differ only in how the per-layer geometry costs are combined:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Arm & Write cost $R_W$ & Realization cost $R_E$\\
\midrule
A: concentration permitted & $\sum_l g_l$ & $\sum_l e_l$\\
B: spread preference & $\sqrt{\sum_l g_l^2}$ & $\sqrt{\sum_l e_l^2}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both arms use
\[
 J_{\rm base}^{(a)}(D)=J_{\rm native}(D)
 +\lambda_W R_W^{(a)}(D)+\lambda_E R_E^{(a)}(D).
 \label{eq:base}
\]
The initial, unvalidated profile fixes $\lambda_W=\lambda_E=0.1$.
These are prospective coefficients, not values selected on official
paraphrases or locality outcomes. The native norm in Eq.~\eqref{eq:native}
remains identical between arms.

Arm A does not charge an additional convex across-layer penalty merely
because fixed total cost is concentrated: for nonnegative $g_l$ with
fixed $\sum_lg_l$, its write penalty is unchanged. Its group norm can
favor sparsity; it does not require sparsity or designate a layer in
advance. Arm B lowers the write penalty when that same total cost is
more evenly distributed. These statements concern the geometry costs,
not equal edit efficacy or a theorem about the optimal allocation.
Layers differ in their task gradients, anchors, realizability, and
history. Either arm may still select a solution dominated by one layer.
In particular, the common quadratic hidden-feedback cost $C_h$ can itself
favor some distributions over others. Arm A removes only the geometry
norm's discount for splitting an identical-cost action across layers;
it does not make the complete objective globally neutral to allocation.

No entropy target, mandatory layer quota, top-$k$ preselection, or
per-layer gain normalization is added. The native-profile learning rate
is constant from the first update. In particular, Adam's
coordinatewise adaptation does not itself establish a dynamic allocation
policy or guarantee preservation.

Norms are evaluated from stable factorizations where possible, with
the zero subgradient selected at an exactly zero norm. Floating-point
negative energies consistent with roundoff are distinguished from a
failed positive-semidefinite or solve check. This version uses no root-norm smoothing. Adding an epsilon or another
smoothing term changes the method, especially near zero initialization,
and requires a separate method revision rather than merely logging it.

All functional physical feedback, memory policy, native inputs, optimizer
settings, and stopping rules are shared. This arm definition supersedes
the old v5 A/B distinction based on the presence or absence of a
preservation term. In v6, \emph{both} arms use the same past preservation
term whenever eligible memory exists.

The design does not rule out a weak-edit or zero-edit optimum when
preservation costs outweigh the task signal. It does not repair such a
result by an unreported gain multiplier. Native loss, actual physical
loss, payload norms, realized action, and write cost must therefore be
reported separately.

\section{Physical feedback from current native sentences}
At a physical pulse, use the same trainable $D$ but no artificial hidden
injection. Instead, materialize all eligible-layer weights as
\[
 W_{{\rm phys},l}^{32}
 =W_{t,l}^{32}+\operatorname{cast}_{32}\!
   \left(D_l^{64}(P_l^{64})^\top\right).
 \label{eq:physical}
\]
The full physical graph includes all current request columns, all edited
layers, all token positions, and input-direction gradients. A single
linear operation uses each materialized weight. Gradients propagate to
$D$ through the declared FP64 geometry and FP32 cast. $P$ is frozen by
definition; it is not an omitted derivative approximation.

The teacher is the \emph{current} native joint forward at the same $D$,
detached only on the teacher side. For a selected current cohort $I$,
let $h^{\rm virt}_{lrc}$ be its post-injection subject block output and
$h^{\rm phys}_{lrc}$ its physical counterpart. With native rewrite
context weights $w_{rc}$ and target length $T_{rc}$, set
\begin{align}
 C_h(I)&=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{r\in I}\frac12
 \sum_l\sum_c w_{rc}
 \frac{\norm{h^{\rm phys}_{lrc}-\sg(h^{\rm virt}_{lrc})}^2}{a_{lr}^2},\\
 C_d(I)&=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{r\in I}\sum_c w_{rc}\frac1{T_{rc}}
 \sum_{j=1}^{T_{rc}}
 \KL\left(\sg(p^{\rm virt}_{rcj})\,\Vert\,p^{\rm phys}_{rcj}\right),\\
 C_K(I)&=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{r\in I}
 \KL\left(p^{\rm phys}_{K,r}\,\Vert\,p^0_{K,r}\right),\\
 C_{\rm cur}(I)&=\beta_h C_h(I)+\beta_d C_d(I)+\lambda_K C_K(I).
 \label{eq:currentfeedback}
\end{align}
Use $\beta_h=\beta_d=0.1$ in the initial profile. $C_d$ uses full
vocabulary probabilities at the exact native NLL readout and target
positions. $C_K$ uses the native KL input and lookup. Its direction is
physical-current $\Vert$ entry. Distillation deliberately uses the
opposite ordering, teacher $\Vert$ student; these are different losses.
No extra physical target NLL replaces the main virtual native NLL.

The hidden loss compares total block outputs, not only $U_lk_l$.
Consequently it includes lower-layer propagation and nonlinear
interaction. The physical student applies every request's weight update
to every selected sentence, exposing cross-request interference that
independent subject injections cannot represent by themselves.

The teacher is neither a permanently frozen zero-edit teacher nor a
target from an independently optimized layer. It is refreshed at each
pulse from the current jointly optimized subject interventions. Teacher
gradients are not reused in subsequent updates. These pulses provide
sampled feedback, not an assertion of perfect subject-target realization
or global nonlinear alignment.

\section{Past preservation and the finite Adam schedule}
Memory contains only already encountered native sentences, a detached
admission-entry KL teacher, and per-context target NLL measured under the
actual committed model for the latest retained edit of that identity.
For an eligible past cohort $J$,
\begin{align}
 C_{\rm past}(J)
 &=\frac1{|J|}\sum_{r\in J}\left[
 \lambda_K\KL(p^{\rm phys}_{K,r}\Vert p^{\rm admit}_{K,r})
 +\sum_c w_{rc}\frac12\pos{L^{\rm phys}_{N,rc}-b^{\rm commit}_{rc}}^2
 \right]. \label{eq:past}
\end{align}
The first teacher is the model at admission entry and remains fixed
while the identity is resident; the NLL baseline follows its latest
retained committed target. They are distinct.
Future requests and official evaluation P/N examples are excluded.

At batch entry, make a seeded random partition $I_1,\ldots,I_4$ of
all $B$ current requests, without repetition. Select once a past set
$S$ of size $M=\min(16,B,\text{eligible memory size})$ from a reservoir
of capacity 128, then partition it into disjoint $J_1,\ldots,J_4$.
Each selected request contributes its complete native bundle. Empty
partitions contribute zero. Candidate numbers 5, 10, 15, and 20 receive
physical feedback before their Adam updates:
\[
 J_q=J_{\rm base}^{(a)}+
 \frac{|I_j|}{B}C_{\rm cur}(I_j)
 +\frac{|J_j|}{M}C_{\rm past}(J_j),
 \qquad q=5j,\quad j=1,\ldots,4.
 \label{eq:pulse}
\]
The past term is zero when $M=0$; other candidates use $J_{\rm base}^{(a)}$
only. The cohort weights sum to one across the four pulses separately
for current and past data. There is no factor-four importance multiplier.
This is an explicit finite feedback schedule with moving detached
teachers, \emph{not} exact descent on one fixed global objective.

Use ordinary Adam in absolute $D$ coordinates, with
$(\beta_1,\beta_2)=(0.9,0.999)$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, weight decay zero,
and all moments reset at batch entry. Candidate $q$ evaluates $D^{q-1}$ for $q=1,\ldots,25$.
For $q\leq24$, accumulate the entire logical-batch gradient and make one
update with
\[
 \gamma_q=\gamma_{\rm native},\qquad q=1,\ldots,24,\qquad
 \nabla_D\{B J_q(D^{q-1})\}.
\]
The initial experiment uses $\gamma_{\rm native}=0.1$ from update 1;
there is no learning-rate warmup. Other model profiles retain their
declared native constant learning rate.
Multiplying the entire mean objective by $B$ gives the declared logical
SUM gradient, including auxiliary terms. Microbatching cannot change
normalization or introduce extra optimizer steps. The scale matters
for Adam epsilon and must not be silently discarded as ``equivalent.''
Apply the native individual clamp after every update. Clamp activity does
not alter the learning rate or introduce an acceptance gate. Candidate 25
has no backward or update and evaluates the final $D^{24}$.

There is no line search, loss-threshold early exit, or quality-based
rejection of a finite final candidate. Numerical integrity checks
(nonfinite values, invalid solves, shape or commit mismatches) remain
mandatory. Fixed-budget completion is not a convergence certificate.

\section{Sequential commit, audits, and implementation boundaries}
Candidate 25 receives a full-current physical forward without backward,
using Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical}. This audits every current native bundle,
captures actual full-context subject keys, and measures per-context NLL
for memory admission. Commit the very same materialized weight tensors.
There is no final basis re-solve, current-key rewrite, residual division
by remaining layers, post-fit gain adjustment, or hidden norm rescaling.

Append the actual committed full-context key moments to history once
per encountered request:
\[
 H_{t+1,l}=H_{tl}+\sum_{r,c}\alpha_{rc}
 k^{\rm commit}_{lrc}(k^{\rm commit}_{lrc})^\top.
\]
These are keys under the fully materialized candidate, including
lower-layer effects. Do not append pooled-key outer products or append
once per pulse. Historical key staleness remains a limitation of this
history statistic. Memory follows the v5 seeded Algorithm R reservoir
over unique fact identities. Current identities are excluded from past
preservation. A resident repeated fact, or a readmitted fact, receives
its latest target and actual-commit NLL baseline; an existing admission
KL teacher is retained while that identity remains resident. Shared-token
teacher ownership and eviction follow v5, and admission has no quality
gate. The companion machine-readable contract specifies the inherited
identity, shared-teacher, and re-admission rules.

At minimum, record the following for every batch:
\begin{itemize}
\item Input identities and order; all eligible layers; context weights,
      lookup positions, target tokens, native readouts, dtypes, seeds,
      hyperparameters, source/model/tokenizer identifiers, and arm.
\item Per-candidate native NLL, current $\Vert$ entry KL, nonsquared
      norm, both geometry costs, and per-layer/group norm and clamp use.
      Record pulse cohorts, detached-teacher identity, physical feedback,
      actual learning rate, and optimizer update count.
\item Terminal virtual/physical native losses, same-layer hidden
      realization error, weight-update norms, solve residuals, history
      and memory transitions, and exact evaluated-to-committed equality.
\item Post-commit evaluation R/P/N with denominators, pre-edit outcomes,
      strict/token metrics where available, and cohort-at-write versus
      cumulative results. Evaluation never feeds optimization.
\end{itemize}
These diagnostics are observations. They are not acceptance gates that
select convenient layers or suppress difficult batches. The two arms
share all diagnostics and evaluation schedules.

An implementation should validate gradients and loss reductions on a
small deterministic case before timing a representative batch. Checks
must cover subject lookup, KL direction, native readout, logical-batch
SUM accumulation, $G+E$ identity, physical backward into $D$, detached
teachers, empty/small partitions, history single admission, and commit
equality. The specification does not authorize changing the training
sentences or using official paraphrases for hyperparameter selection.

The method is defined for arbitrary positive $B$ and any compatible
model/benchmark profile. Fixed constants such as 128 and 16 are memory
profile choices, not assumptions that $B=100$. The profile must identify
each writable projection and its corresponding native injection site;
architectures without that correspondence require an explicit adapter.

\section{Computational contract, interpretation, and open questions}
Every native candidate reuses an unchanged prefix before the first
subject intervention. The physical graph has a different reuse boundary:
all-token weight modifications invalidate computation from the first
modified projection onward. A native-prefix cache cannot simply be
reused as a physical suffix cache. Cache keys include batch-entry model
state, token IDs/masks/positions, and the relevant graph boundary.

Teacher hidden states and selected full-vocabulary logits can be captured
from the existing native candidate forward. This requires no additional
teacher model forward. Materialize each physical weight once per pulse
and share it across physical microbatches. Compute full-vocabulary heads
only at positions required by the unchanged native losses. Remove only
padding proven irrelevant under preserved masks and positions. Geometry
is FP64 and the reference model/materialized weights are FP32.

With $B$ current and $M$ selected past requests, counts in complete native
request bundles are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Path & Forward bundle exposures & Backward bundle exposures\\
\midrule
25 native candidates & $25B$ & $24B$\\
Four physical pulses combined & $B+M$ & $B+M$\\
Terminal full-current physical audit & $B$ & $0$\\
\midrule
Total & $27B+M$ & $25B+M$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are logical exposures, not kernel calls, equal-cost model forwards,
FLOP counts, or a measured runtime ratio to a baseline. They exclude entry
anchor/key extraction, teachers and cache construction, geometry solves,
history/memory bookkeeping, and external evaluation. Joint subject
backward and physical-weight backward have different costs. Batch-size
and sequence-length effects must be measured rather than inferred from
the exposure totals.

The intended scientific interpretation is narrow. Subject injection
preserves the native factual-edit learning mechanism while simultaneous
optimization captures within-request, cross-layer interaction. Fixed
full-context geometry exposes entry-key cross-request coupling cheaply.
Occasional physical feedback additionally sees actual nonlinear
propagation and shared-weight interference, while the exact-commit rule
eliminates a last-minute writer mismatch. None of these establishes that
all local targets are perfectly realizable.

Several uncertainties remain explicit. Sparse pulses may be insufficient
to align the final physical model with the current virtual teacher.
Fixed $P$ may be poorly matched to changed keys. Native-only memory is a
proxy for preservation, not coverage of all neighboring knowledge.
The provisional coefficients and finite Adam budget may underfit or
overedit. The cross-layer sum and Euclidean cost shapes are policy
choices whose effect must be separated from optimizer behavior. A high
locality preference rate with weak editing is not by itself a successful
allocation result, and a high virtual native score is not a substitute
for physical paraphrase performance.

The two-arm comparison changes only the across-layer cost shape. Any
additional optimizer, pulse-frequency, reference-data, or coefficient
ablation is a separate experiment. This source is a method handoff;
implementation qualification and measured speed/quality results must be
reported subsequently. The first experiment runs both arms through 500
sequential edits; this execution limit does not restrict the method's
logical batch size, model, or benchmark.
